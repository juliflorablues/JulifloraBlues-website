Lantana Camara:The Blooms of Absolute Conquest

By Kavya Dhimmar · August 2026

Lantana camara, a native of the tropics of Americas, is a flowering species from the verbena family. This plant was brought into India in the early 1800s by the British as an ornamental and hedge plant. In Goa, this plant was brought by the Portugese. The leaves are broad, oval shaped and have a strong odour when crushed..

The flowers, which come in multiple colours such as red, yellow, white, pink and orange, are tubular and each have 4 petals. The flowers smell like tutti frutti with a peppery undertone. The flowers change colours as they mature and age.

The fruit is a berry-like drupe (fleshy fruit with a hard, woody endocarp). It turns dark green from purple at maturity. There are dense patches of hard spikes on the rind which if ingested can cause serious damage to the respiratory tract. Seed and vegetative reproduction both can occur. Each plant can produce 12,000 fruits..

WHY IS IT A PROBLEM?

It adapts quickly making it able to thrive in a wide variety of ecosystems. It falls under the world's ten worst invasive species.

Lantana has learned to tolerate warmer temperatures and more moisture in India while India's own native plants fail in this aspect. It harms the biodiversity of India. Lantana also acts as a fuel for uncontrollable forest fires. Its dry, woody stems act as flammable timber. Also, since the plant is highly fire resistant, the weed regrows quickly first after a fire which prevents the other plants in the forests from recovering.

Its invasion results in scarcity of the native forage plants. The wild herbivores eat the leaves which can induce allergies on their muzzles. Some cases of extensive feeding on the plant has resulted in diarrhoea, liver failure and even death..

HOW IT SPREADS.

Lantana gets propagated in multiple ways. It is mainly dispersed by frugivorous birds, monkeys, bears, etc. It also possesses the capability to grow from its root-stock, and nodes (i.e. vegetative propagation).

TACKLING IT

The plant cannot be merely cut above ground, it needs to be uprooted to prevent aggressive regrowth from the root. Even with weeding, the recovery of native grasses remains limited, therefore it is recommended to uproot Lantana plants, followed by weeding and seeding of native grasses for effective return in forests. Controlled fires can be one way to destroy seeds and help control rates of re-invasion of Lantana. But use of fire to control Lantana must be carefully orchestrated and managed..
